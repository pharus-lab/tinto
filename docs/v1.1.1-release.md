# Tinto v1.1.1 release record

Wider local writing checks and an optional writing coach. This record follows the format of [the v1.1 record](v1.1-release.md); a checklist item is complete only when its result is recorded against the exact source and artifact tested.

## Why

Version 1.1.0 showed too little of what Harper finds. On 33 typical errors, Harper detected 12 and Tinto showed 7: it let through 7 of Harper's 21 kinds of finding, and it looked for a kind named `WordBoundary`, which harper.js 3.2.0 calls `BoundaryError`. The other 21 errors are beyond a rule-based checker: comma splices and run-on sentences, apostrophes and homophones such as "its" and "lets", agreement across a phrase, wordiness, redundancy, and sentences that are hard to follow.

## Release decisions

- **Version 1.1.1**, as the writer chose, although it adds features (SemVer would suggest 1.2.0). The update notice treats it as newer than 1.1.0.
- **Local checks show every Harper error kind**: word choice, usage, word order, idioms (eggcorns, malapropisms and nonstandard forms), word boundaries and spacing, plus reviewed rules from Harper's Miscellaneous kind (`ThenThan`, `Everyone`, `SinceDuration`) and double comparatives (`MoreBetter`, `AdjectiveDoubleDegree`). Unknown kinds stay closed by default.
- **Style hints are a separate switch**, off by default: Harper's Redundancy, Readability (including long sentences) and Style kinds, and `VeryUnique`, `CondenseAllThe` and `RedundantAcronyms`. They join English grammar checks only.
- **House-style rules are never shown**: Oxford comma either way, dashes, ellipsis character, title case, spelled-out numbers, avoiding contractions, French spaces, quotation style and orthographic consistency.
- **Harper keeps Apply.** Its corrections are deterministic and local; each still needs the writer's click.
- **The writing coach is suggestion-only.** It is off by default, marked with sparkles under *Advanced* in the Writing checks sidebar and in Settings → Writing. It runs only with Check writing, never while typing, on the chosen wording of the open document or linked section, up to about 10,000 words (`COACH_LIMIT`, 60,000 characters), in parts of about 2,000 words (`COACH_PART`). Notes have no Apply; the writer edits each passage. Because nothing is inserted, the authorship record and `generatedCharacters` are unchanged.
- **The coach uses the AI service of Settings → Writing** (renamed from *Automatic translation*): the same service, key and model as Translate, with a strict JSON schema and `store: false` where the service supports it. A note whose quote is not found verbatim in its paragraph is dropped and counted, so Tinto never marks text the model did not see.
- **The coach sits below the local results** in the Writing checks sidebar, under *Advanced*, with no reminders of what it does; its status line names the model.
- **The toolbars are customizable** in Settings → Appearance → Toolbar: each of the six document-bar tools and nine formatting-bar tools can be hidden, and all start shown. Hidden tools keep their keyboard shortcuts; a divider shows only between two groups that still have a tool.
- **Spanish is Spanish (Mexico).** The language code stays `es`, so documents, translations and published hreflang are unchanged. The spelling dictionary is `dictionary-es-mx` 2.0.0 (GPL-3.0, LGPL-3.0 or MPL-1.1), which accepted Mexican words the general dictionary rejected (güey, chido, jitomate, tianguis, pozole, Michoacán, Oaxaca, Querétaro) and rejects only vosotros forms (habláis, tenéis). Translations into Spanish ask for Mexican Spanish. The coach follows US English, British English or Mexican Spanish norms and explains in the document's language.

The new tests:

- `tests/writing-engine.test.mjs`: the new kinds appear with readable names ("should of", "taller then", "alot", "for all intensive purposes", "more better", "discussed about", a space before a period); style hints only with their switch and only with grammar; house-style rules never.
- `tests/writing-coach.test.mjs`: instructions for each dialect and feedback language; the strict schema; exact anchoring, tolerant of curly quotes and collapsed spaces; dropped invented, misplaced, duplicate or malformed notes; size limits; parts of whole blocks; OpenAI, OpenRouter and Claude requests (strict schema, `store: false`, `require_parameters`, `output_config`) and their review-specific error messages.
- `tests/writing-desktop.test.mjs`:
  - **Defaults:** the coach and style hints start off; without a key the coach's switch is disabled and says where to add one; with the switch off nothing is sent.
  - **A review:** with the switch on, Check writing sends only the chosen wording of the scope; notes show their kind, explanation and suggestion, with **no Apply and no replacement menu**; the passage button selects the text and leaves focus in the manuscript; the context menu (Shift+F10) offers Dismiss only; Dismiss leaves the text unchanged.
  - **Afterward:** an edit clears the paragraph's notes until Check again; a failing service is reported without hiding Harper's results; turning the coach off removes its notes; the language menu names Spanish (Mexico).
  - **Order:** the local results come before the coach.
- `tests/navigation.test.mjs`: every tool starts shown; hiding Translate, Bold, Italic, Underline and Markdown removes them and leaves one divider; Cmd/Ctrl+B still bolds; the choice survives a restart and can be undone.

### Writer's check with a real key

On 2026-11-23, with a temporary OpenAI key (deleted afterwards, with the test profile that stored it encrypted), `gpt-6.2-astra` reviewed three sample texts with typical errors:

| Text | Notes | Dropped | Time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| English (US), two paragraphs | 12 | 0 | 20.5 s |
| English (UK), one paragraph | 4 | 0 | 8.7 s |
| Spanish (Mexico), two paragraphs | 7 | 0 | 13.1 s |

Every note quoted its passage exactly. The coach found comma splices, "me and him was", "they was", "its"/"it's", "didnt", a doubled subject, inconsistent agreement with "data", a long interruption before the verb and wordy phrases; in British English it kept British spelling and single quotation marks; in Spanish it explained in Spanish (impersonal *haber*, *dijistes*/*llegastes*, *la razón de porque… es porque*, commas before *pero* and around an aside) and did not flag *tianguis* or *jitomates*. The writer then used the coach in the app, on an English and a Spanish document, and approved the release.
